## The three answers, one sentence each

1. **What the models are worth:** nothing over the bookmaker's price — five
   independent routes agree, and the best-measured model in the repository is
   the price itself.

nothing measurable.**

## Both caveats on the `w → 1.0` recommendation

1. **Three effective folds of five, and they are adjacent.** The 5-of-5 count
   printed by the harness is over *all* rows passed in; the **priced** subset
   spans fewer, measured at 3 of 5 for the 2026-08-07 audit's 1,710 priced
   matches. Three adjacent folds support a statement about *this period*, not
   about time in general.
2. **The era's book mix was itself atypical.** Odds collection began
   2026-02, and in that one era the corruption gate rejected **4,836 1X2
   books** — Bet365 at 92% implausible, Pinnacle at 26%. So the population the
   intervals describe is unusual, not merely narrow. Lifting this needs a
   *second* era collected with the gate in force from the start, so its books
   are clean by construction rather than by filtering.

## The Brier correction is no evidence either way

Paired on n=479: **Δ = +0.0001, t = +0.07**. The earlier reported Brier gap was
an **unpaired population artefact** — it compared two different sets of
matches. Pairing it removed the gap entirely.

**This is not a vindication of the model.** A t of +0.07 is a measurement that
distinguishes nothing. It removed a reason to think the model was *worse* than
the price on that metric; it supplied no reason to think it is as good. The
conclusion in Part B rests on routes 1–4, not on this.

# PART C — win rate against ROI, demonstrated

`measured` 2026-10-23, all 2,388 decided settled picks. Bands are round
numbers chosen to split the population into comparable parts, **not fitted to
outcome** — a boundary tuned on ROI would be the sixteenth fitted threshold in
this repository.

| taken price band | n | **win rate** | **break-even win rate** | gap | flat ROI | 95% CI (flat) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| short (<1.80) | 1,653 | **59.7%** | 62.9% | **−3.2 pts** | −5.19% | [−8.88%, −1.33%] |
| medium (1.80–2.50) | 614 | **46.9%** | 48.9% | **−2.0 pts** | −4.73% | [−12.61%, +3.42%] |
| long (≥2.50) | 121 | **33.1%** | 35.4% | **−2.3 pts** | −6.14% | [−30.83%, +18.41%] |

**Every interval overlaps every other.** The ROI is statistically the same in
all three bands, while the win rate ranges from 33% to 60%.

**The win rate is below break-even in every single band**, by 2.0 to 3.2
points. That gap *is* the loss. It is remarkably stable across a price range
where the win rate nearly doubles.

**Always taking the shortest price available** — one pick per fixture, the
shortest, over all 2,206 fixtures:

* win rate **56.0%** (up from 55.1%)
* average odds **1.74**
* flat ROI **−4.85%** (against −5.12% for the full set)

*Descriptive only.* This is not a strategy the system could have run — it
selects among picks the system actually made, and the shortest price on a
fixture is only known once all of them are recorded. It is here to show the
relationship, not to propose anything.

**Best-line break-even overround: +1.85%.** Any strategy that does not beat
the closing line must overcome this before it makes a penny.

## Why raising the win rate is easy and why it does not help

Niki's question is about picks coming out `win`, so here is the mechanism in
plain language.

The win rate is a **choice of price**, not a measure of skill. Take shorter
prices and the win rate rises automatically: the table above moves from 33% to
60% purely by moving down the price range. You can push it past 80% by betting
only heavy favourites. But the break-even win rate rises *at exactly the same
time*, and slightly faster — because the bookmaker's margin is baked into
every price, and on short prices that margin is a larger share of the thin
profit each win returns.

So the win rate and the bar it must clear move together, and what matters is
only the **gap** between them. Here that gap is −2 to −3 points everywhere,
and it is almost exactly the **+1.85% overround** plus a little. The system is
not losing because it picks wrong. It is losing because it pays the margin and
has no edge to pay it with. **Changing the win rate changes which price you
pay the margin at; it does not stop you paying it.**

Across 8 splits × 2 populations, the tables make **1,000 pairwise 95%-interval

> the eleven is treated as a finding, because there is nothing to correct

## The only conclusion permitted here

> **Does the model's own edge estimate rank outcomes at all? No.**

It is decisive, and it is decisive in the strongest available form:

* the **highest**-edge quartile has the **worst** ROI (−9.26% vs −5.07%) and
  the **worst** CLV (−1.48% vs +0.24%);
* every ROI interval overlaps every other, so the ordering is not even
  reliably *inverted* — it is absent;
* across the whole exercise, **11 of 1,000 comparisons separate where 50 are
  expected by chance.**

**Selectivity cannot help, because there is nothing to be selective on.** A
selection rule needs a quantity that orders outcomes; this population contains
no such quantity, and that includes the model's own edge, its confidence, the
price, the league, the market, the lead time and the review's verdict.

Were a ranking ever found, acting on it would require a **pre-registered
out-of-sample test** — not a table like this one. That is not a caveat added
for form: fifteen thresholds in this repository were fitted on tables like
this one, and the measurement that closed them is the multiplicity figure
above.

## The minimum detectable effect, at 80% power, one-sided

| captured-close subset | 170 | 170 | 1.00 | 170 | **+15.94%** |

## So what size of edge can still be hiding?

**Against the +1.85% break-even overround:**

| population | MDE | shortfall |
|---|---|---|
| paper + valid evidence | +5.60% | **3.0× too coarse** |
| live + valid evidence | +8.51% | **4.6× too coarse** |
| all settled (pooled across two eras) | +4.84% | **2.6× too coarse** |
| captured-close subset | +15.94% | **8.6× too coarse** |

**An edge of up to about +5.6% could be hiding in the paper record and this
measurement would not see it.** That is three times the size of the edge that
would actually matter. The honest statement is therefore *not* "there is no
edge" — it is **"the model has no measurable edge, and the measurement is too
coarse to exclude one three times larger than the one worth having."**

Those are different claims, and Part B is why the first is still the
conclusion: five routes agree on direction, and one of them (the blend sweep)
is a *monotone* result that does not depend on this power calculation at all.

## Stage 16's finding, restated

The 500-observation closing-line checkpoint was **over-specified about
twenty-nine-fold**. The MODEL series' upper bound was **+0.107%** against the
**+1.85%** requirement — so **~17 observations suffice** to exclude a
decision-relevant effect on that axis, and 500 were specified.

The MODEL series now stands at n=142 with mean −0.273%, CI [−0.8%, +0.2%].
**The upper bound is still an order of magnitude below break-even.** More
closing lines buy precision on a question that is already answered.

## Is any open question answerable by waiting?

| question | answerable by waiting? |
|---|---|
| "does the model beat the price on forecast accuracy" | **already answered** — waiting adds folds, not direction |
| "does the model have a +1.85%-scale ROI edge" | **yes, in ~15 months** — on a question four other routes have answered |
| "does the model's edge estimate rank outcomes" | **yes, slowly** — but Part D's answer is already unambiguous at n=1,346 |
| **H1 — does the model anticipate line movement** | **NO — `UNADDRESSABLE BY SELF-OBSERVATION`.** The system cannot observe its own effect on a line it does not move. Returned n=4, power 0.073. |
| **what `config/config.yaml` held at each past bump** | **NO — `UNADDRESSABLE` in principle.** The file is untracked, so it has no history. Today's file is sha256-identical to the example, so probably none differed — *luck, recorded as luck.* |

> **If the honest answer is that more measurement of the same thing changes
> nothing, say it in one sentence:** more measurement of the same thing
> changes nothing — fifteen months of further collection would tighten an
> interval around a conclusion that five independent routes already agree on,
> and the two genuinely open questions are both unanswerable by self-
> observation rather than under-powered.

# PART G — the options, with evidence and cost. No recommendation.

## 1. Keep measuring as-is

* **Buys (Part E):** the MDE tightens from +5.60% to about +5.2% by 11-01, and
  reaching the +1.85% break-even needs **14.7 more months**. Halving the
  current bound needs **5.4 months**.
* **Costs (Part F):** 64.3 runner min/day, ~400 TheOddsAPI credits/month
  against a ~50-credit margin, 67.2 API-Football calls/day against a 100/day
  cap with 7 calls of worst-day headroom, 1.20 Telegram msgs/day, ~10 manual
  dispatches/month.
* **What would have to be true for it to work:** that the conclusion is wrong
  and an edge of +2% to +5% exists. Five routes say otherwise.
* **How we would know, and when:** 2027-12 at the current rate — and even then
  only about ROI, not about why.

## 2. Reduce the pick set

> **UNAVAILABLE.** Part D closes it.

The measurement that closes it: **11 of 1,000 pairwise comparisons separate
where 50 are expected by chance**, and the model's highest-edge quartile is
its worst performer on both ROI (−9.26% vs −5.07%) and CLV (−1.48% vs
+0.24%). A selection rule needs a quantity that orders outcomes; none of the
seven candidates examined does.

* **What would have to be true:** some quantity, measured before the fixture,
  orders outcomes. Seven were tested and none does.
* **How we would know:** a ranking would have to appear first in a descriptive
  table and then survive a **pre-registered out-of-sample test**. The first
  step has not happened.

## 3. Remove the Claude review

**Measured 2026-10-23, paired on CLV, the only axis on which it can be
measured:**

| | figure |
|---|---|
| paired observations | **132 on 132 fixtures** |
| model CLV | −0.460% |
| final CLV | −0.356% |
| **final − model** | **+0.104%**, 95% CI [−0.2%, +0.5%] |
| by action | `KEEP` delta +0.00% (n=72, same bet); `CHANGE` delta **+0.47%** (n=77) |
| `CHANGE` picks that are negative-EV | **440 of 510 = 86%** |

**The sign has flipped since the last reading** (−0.101% then, +0.104% now, on
a larger n), and both sit well inside the interval. **The most informative
fact about this measurement is that its sign is unstable** — it is no evidence
either way.

> **A paired ROI comparison of review-versus-model cannot be computed, and
> this paper will not quote one.** There is no `model_odds` column: the price
> at which the model's own selection could have been taken is not stored. Any
> paired ROI figure has to borrow the *final* pick's price for the model's
> leg — which is wrong **exactly on the 413 `CHANGE` picks the comparison is
> about**. A figure computed that way comes out at +0.534% [−3.36%, +4.41%];
> it is reported here only to say that it is **`UNEVALUATED`, not +0.534%.**

* **What removing it saves:** 21.4 reviews/day of shared Claude pool,
  a share of the 43.0 min/run `daily-picks` duration, and the fallback-API
  exposure. The monetary saving is **zero** — the subscription is fixed-price.
* **What removing it loses:** a measured +0.104% CLV whose sign is unstable,
  and the 86%-negative-EV `CHANGE` behaviour stops.
* **What would have to be true for keeping it:** that the review adds CLV.
  Two readings of opposite sign on n=132 do not support that.
* **How we would know:** the CLV series needs roughly **17 observations** to
  exclude a decision-relevant effect (Stage 16). It has 132 and shows none.

## 4. Change the question — line movement rather than outcome

* **What H1 returned:** **n = 4, power 0.073.**
* **Its status:** `UNADDRESSABLE BY SELF-OBSERVATION` — the system cannot
  observe its own effect on a line it does not move. This is a disposition,
  not a sample-size problem.
* **Cost of a second attempt:** **168 TheOddsAPI credits and eight days**, from
  a pool with ~50 credits of monthly margin. The collection defects are now
  fixed, and `odds_snapshots` holds 178,828 rows.
* **What it could conclude:** whether prices move toward the model's view.
  **What it could not:** whether that is the model anticipating information or
  the model agreeing with whatever already moved the line — which is the part
  that is unaddressable.
* **The honest risk:** `h1-collect` cost **221.3 min/run**, the most expensive
  thing in the repository, and five runs produced n=4 and no analysis.

## 5. Change the inputs — markets and data the system does not hold

| candidate | cost to obtain | does the record suggest it carries information? |
|---|---|---|
| **Closing lines at scale** (the binding limit on CLV) | the capture rate is 13.0%; `late`+`missing` is 1,125 of 1,325. Fixing it is a scheduling problem, not a credit problem | **Already answered on the axis it informs** — MODEL upper bound +0.107% vs +1.85%, 29× over-specified |
| **Asian handicap / totals lines** | a provider that supplies them; not in the current free tier | **No evidence either way** — never priced, so `UNEVALUATED` |
| **Lineups / injuries** | already reach the Claude prompt; not in any fit | `UNEVALUATED` as a *feature* — they influence the review, whose own effect is unmeasurable (option 3) |
| **Weather** | — | **Answered: no.** The producer was deleted 2026-10-20; its 8/8 feature vectors were byte-identical and reached no fit, no inference and no prompt |
| **xG** | already held and already used (Poisson fits on xG at ≥50% coverage) | in use; not separately measured |

**Nothing in the record suggests an unheld input carries information**, and the
one input whose reach was actually audited (weather) turned out to reach
nothing at all. That is not an argument that no such input exists — it is the
observation that this project has never found one, and has twice found the
opposite.

## 6. Stop predicting; keep the pipeline, or stop altogether

* **Keep as an instrument.** The pipeline's measured, working outputs are: a
  **42,591-match** database (42,410 with a result, though xG reaches only
  1,351 of them), a de-vigged consensus price that **beats
  every model in the repository**, a closing-line capture path, and an audit
  apparatus whose two enumerable series stand at **13 comment-blind scans**
  and **5 `sys.path` entry-point defects** — the thirteenth and the fifth both
  found this week, the fifth while writing this paper. Vacuous checks are
  **not cited as a count**: the individual ones are in the ledger but the
  series has never been enumerated, and a count that cannot be enumerated is
  not a count. Cost: the 64.3 min/day in Part F, less whatever the prediction
  path contributes.
* **Stop altogether.** Cost: zero. Loses the instrument and the record.
* **What would have to be true for "keep as an instrument":** that the
  non-prediction outputs are worth 64.3 runner min/day and ~10 manual
  dispatches a month. That is a judgement about Niki's time, not a
  measurement, and this paper does not make it.
